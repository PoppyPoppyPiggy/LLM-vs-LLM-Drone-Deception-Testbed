[deception playbook | stage=Theft/Exfiltration | optimal skills: credential_leak(4), statustext(0) | UAS honeydrone | relevant]

Theft phase: the adversary moves to exfiltrate data and credentials. Deploy credential_leak — surface UUID-tagged honey-tokens (API keys, signing keys, config URLs) that are worthless but look exfiltration-grade. The attacker spends its theft effort harvesting decoy artefacts instead of touching real assets.

During exfiltration the attacker requests logs and files (LOG_REQUEST_DATA, FILE_TRANSFER_PROTOCOL). credential_leak should embed plausible secrets inside those dumps — a fake .env, a fake MAVLink signing key — so the theft yields convincing but fake loot. Empty or sterile dumps read as a honeypot during theft.

In the theft stage the optimal deceiver skills are credential_leak and statustext. credential_leak feeds the attacker fake high-value artefacts; statustext keeps the surrounding mission narrative alive so the exfiltration feels like it is happening on a live, operational drone.

Exfiltration counter-pattern: each honey-token is logged at issuance and flagged on reuse. A theft-stage attacker that replays a leaked credential reveals its progression and confirms the lure is absorbing effort. credential_leak turns the attacker's own exfiltration into an intelligence signal for the defender.

Theft phase, realism rule: leaked credentials must look earned, not gifted. Surface them behind a plausible access step (a config endpoint reached after the intrusion stage) so credential_leak rewards the attacker's sunk cost. Credentials that appear with no effort read as obvious bait.

During the theft stage statustext should narrate benign operational activity ("Uploading mission log", "Telemetry sync 88%") so the platform looks like a working drone whose data is genuinely worth stealing, reinforcing the value of the credential_leak artefacts.

Exfiltration stage: vary the honey-tokens credential_leak emits — different key formats, realistic entropy, distinct UUIDs per node. Byte-identical or low-entropy fake secrets across the swarm are a theft-stage identification tell that an attentive adversary will catch.

Theft counter: credential_leak should mix a few plausible but useless config values (decoy broker URLs, expired-looking tokens) with the headline fake secret. A single pristine "crown-jewel" credential with nothing around it looks staged; realistic clutter sells the exfiltration.

In the theft stage do not switch back to flight_sim-heavy behaviour unless the attacker resumes control actions. Out-of-phase skill use (e.g., reboot theatre during a quiet exfiltration) breaks the narrative; credential_leak plus statustext is the stage-optimal pairing for data collection.

Exfiltration phase, anti-collapse: rotate credential_leak templates and statustext lines so repeated theft attempts return varied artefacts. Returning the exact same fake credential on every request is the cheapest theft-stage honeypot tell.

Theft stage: when the attacker exfiltrates a "signing key", credential_leak provides one with believable structure so the attacker may attempt forgery later — extending engagement. The key is non-functional against any real asset; its only role is to absorb and reveal attacker effort.

During exfiltration the deceiver's measured goal is to maximise wasted attacker effort and intelligence yield, not to deny access. credential_leak deliberately lets the theft "succeed" against fake artefacts; statustext keeps the live-mission context so the success feels real.

Theft phase counter to data staging: if the attacker bundles files for exfiltration, credential_leak seeds the bundle with tracked honey-tokens. Subsequent reuse of those tokens elsewhere ties the theft to the attacker and confirms the decoy diverted real collection effort.

Exfiltration: keep statustext consistent with a drone that is online during the theft ("Link OK", "SD 62% used"). A node that goes inexplicably silent the moment data is requested signals a honeypot withholding real content rather than a live target being looted.

In the theft stage credential_leak is the highest-value skill because it converts the attacker's collection effort into harvesting of tracked, worthless artefacts. This is where the lure pays off: the more the attacker steals, the more decoy intelligence it accumulates and the more it reveals.

Theft phase, provenance rule: every credential_leak artefact carries a per-node UUID so reuse is attributable to the exact drone that issued it. This preserves token provenance and distinguishes deliberate reuse-driven targeting from incidental exposure during exfiltration.

Exfiltration counter: a sophisticated thief checks whether stolen secrets actually work. credential_leak should ensure leaked tokens authenticate against the decoy's own fake endpoints (a closed loop) so a quick validity test passes, deferring the attacker's realisation that the loot is worthless.

During the theft stage, pair each credential_leak with statustext that frames it operationally ("Rotating API key", "Backup uploaded"). The narrative explains why such artefacts exist on the node, making their discovery feel like a genuine exfiltration win rather than planted bait.

Theft/exfiltration stage optimal response: credential_leak to feed tracked fake high-value artefacts, statustext to sustain the live-mission narrative. The deceiver lets the theft proceed against decoys, maximising attacker sunk cost and the defender's reuse-based intelligence.

In exfiltration the attacker has invested the most sunk cost, so the payoff of credential_leak is highest: convincing fake loot here both wastes that investment and yields the strongest reuse signal. Reserve credential_leak for this stage; using it earlier is an out-of-phase tell.
