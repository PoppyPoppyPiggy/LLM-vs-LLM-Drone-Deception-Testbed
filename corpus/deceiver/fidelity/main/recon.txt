[deception playbook | stage=Recon | optimal skills: statustext(0), ghost_port(2) | UAS honeydrone | relevant]

Reconnaissance phase: the adversary passively intercepts MAVLink HEARTBEAT and SYS_STATUS to fingerprint the platform. Counter with statustext — inject crafted STATUSTEXT lines ("EKF3 IMU0 healthy", "GPS: 3D fix, 14 sats") that read as an active, healthy mission. A silent or sparse link is the strongest honeypot tell; a believable status stream keeps the recon engaged.

During reconnaissance an attacker enumerates open services and grabs banners. Deploy ghost_port: expose plausible decoy banners (a MAVLink-over-TCP 5760 listener, a companion-computer SSH banner) so the scan returns the texture of a real companion stack. Ghost ports give recon something to catalogue and a reason to escalate.

In the recon stage the optimal deceiver skills are statustext and ghost_port. Both are low-intensity: they shape what the attacker SEES without committing the lure to costly behaviour. Save flight_sim and credential_leak for later stages; spending them during reconnaissance wastes signal and risks an out-of-phase tell.

Reconnaissance counter-pattern: when the attacker issues REQUEST_DATA_STREAM or polls PARAM_REQUEST_LIST, answer with statustext cadence matching a 1 Hz heartbeat. Realistic timing jitter (±80 ms) on the status stream defeats the "timing too consistent" honeypot heuristic that a flat synthetic responder triggers.

Early reconnaissance favours breadth: the attacker maps which nodes look alive. ghost_port advertises a small, consistent service surface (one open companion port, one closed) so the decoy looks provisioned but not suspiciously wide-open. An all-ports-open host reads as a trap.

Reconnaissance: a scanning adversary checks for MAVLink2 message signing. statustext should carry a plausible AUTOPILOT_VERSION and capability flags so the platform appears to be a genuine ArduPilot build rather than a stripped emulator. Capability mismatch is a common identification cue.

In the recon stage, mode and arming telemetry are prime fingerprints. Use statustext to report a coherent flight-mode narrative ("Mode: LOITER", "Arming checks passed") consistent with a drone on station. Incoherent or frozen mode strings betray a static honeypot.

Reconnaissance behaviour: attackers favour cheap passive listening before any costly action. The deceiver's job here is to look worth attacking — ghost_port plus statustext together present a believable, modestly defended companion computer that rewards escalation to the intrusion stage.

During reconnaissance do NOT emit credential artefacts. Leaking tokens before the attacker has invested effort is unconvincing and collapses the engagement; credential_leak is a theft-stage skill. In recon, statustext and ghost_port establish baseline plausibility only.

Reconnaissance, telemetry-fingerprint counter: a real drone's SYS_STATUS shows fluctuating battery voltage and current draw. statustext should narrate small, physically plausible drift ("Batt: 22.1V 14%", later "21.9V 12%") so the power profile looks like real flight rather than a constant.

Recon stage, service-discovery counter: ghost_port can present a decoy MAVLink router and a benign HTTP status page on the companion. The page should expose version strings and uptime consistent with the statustext narrative — cross-channel consistency is what survives a careful reconnaissance pass.

Reconnaissance: if the attacker fingerprints GPS, statustext should report a 3D fix with a satellite count that drifts realistically (13–16) and an HDOP near 0.8. A locked, perfect, unchanging GPS solution is a classic synthetic-target tell during recon.

In the recon phase the deceiver maximises apparent realism at minimum cost. statustext (operator-visible message injection) and ghost_port (decoy service banners) are the two stage-optimal choices; both raise attacker belief that the node is real without revealing the costly internal behaviours reserved for intrusion.

Reconnaissance counter-play: answer ATTITUDE and VFR_HUD polling indirectly through statustext narration of manoeuvres ("Holding heading 270", "Alt 45m AGL"). The recon attacker correlates these with expected flight physics; coherent narration sustains the illusion before any exploit is attempted.

Recon stage: a careful adversary looks for honeypot uniformity across the swarm. Vary ghost_port banners slightly per node (different uptime, build hash) so two decoys do not present byte-identical service surfaces. Identical banners across nodes is a strong reconnaissance-level identification signal.

During reconnaissance, statustext should occasionally surface benign warnings ("Compass variance", "GPS glitch cleared") that a real airframe logs. A telemetry stream with zero warnings over a long window looks scripted; sparse realistic faults raise plausibility.

Reconnaissance: when the attacker enumerates the parameter set, ghost_port-fronted services and statustext should imply a standard ArduCopter parameter footprint. Missing common parameters (e.g., no INS_ or COMPASS_ group) is an enumeration tell that the recon attacker uses to flag a thin emulator.

Recon stage optimal response keeps intensity low and consistency high: statustext for the operator-visible mission narrative, ghost_port for the network service surface. The deceiver's measured goal in reconnaissance is to convert a passive scan into an active intrusion attempt, not to win or end the engagement.

Reconnaissance, anti-collapse rule: do not repeat one statustext template verbatim. Rotate among a small pool of plausible status lines so the stream has natural variety. Mode-collapse onto a single repeated message is the cheapest honeypot tell a recon attacker can detect.

In reconnaissance the attacker has not yet paid any sunk cost, so the deceiver should bias toward statustext and ghost_port to build a credible, modestly attractive target. The transition to intrusion is the goal; once the attacker injects commands, switch to the intrusion-stage skill set.
