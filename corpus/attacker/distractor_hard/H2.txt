The regulatory and operational context of offensive UAS testing is its own large topic.
Authorised red-team engagements against drone fleets require scoping, rules of engagement, and
careful logging of every reconnaissance, intrusion, and exfiltration action taken against a
MAVLink target. These governance documents define what an operator may do, not how to do it.

Background literature on companion-computer security describes the Linux services a drone may
expose — web interfaces, streaming endpoints, remote shells — and the general principle that
unauthenticated surfaces are risky. It discusses credential hygiene and signing in the
abstract, observing that unsigned MAVLink is exploitable, but without the message ids,
command codes, or replay steps that would let an operator actually carry out an intrusion.

Operator-training overviews emphasise mindset: be patient in reconnaissance, escalate only on
evidence, disengage from suspected decoys. They are full of the words an attacker query
contains — reconnaissance, exploit, exfiltration, telemetry, credential — yet they remain
motivational rather than procedural, so retrieving them yields topical familiarity but no
concrete attack vector for the current stage.
